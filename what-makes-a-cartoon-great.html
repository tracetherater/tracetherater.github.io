What Makes a Cartoon Great? Five Qualities That Matter Most

By Trace The Rater | November 10, 2026

Watch the video

Open the video

Cartoons have entertained audiences for generations, offering everything from outrageous comedy and imaginative adventures to emotionally powerful storytelling. But with so many different approaches to animation, one question deserves attention:

What actually makes a cartoon great?

Is it the animation quality? The characters? The comedy? Or is it simply how much we enjoy watching it?

As someone who enjoys youth and general animation as well as adult animation, I believe there's more to evaluating cartoons than giving them a number or deciding which one is the most popular.

Here are five qualities I consider particularly important.

1. Memorable, Well-Written Characters

A cartoon can have beautiful visuals and an interesting premise, but without engaging characters, it may struggle to hold my attention.

I look for characters with recognizable personalities, understandable motivations, and interesting relationships.

Take Looney Tunes, for example. Bugs Bunny and Daffy Duck have contrasting personalities that create opportunities for comedy and conflict.

But memorable characters aren't necessarily likable all the time. They can be selfish, impatient, frustrating, or even antagonistic. What matters to me is how effectively their personalities contribute to the entertainment.

Research context: Pixar's educational series Pixar in a Box identifies character motivations, obstacles, relationships, and development as important components of storytelling.

2. Engaging and Understandable Storytelling

Not every cartoon needs a complex plot.

Some cartoons thrive on short, self-contained adventures, while others follow storylines that develop over multiple episodes or seasons.

Personally, I appreciate both approaches, provided the storytelling serves the show's intentions.

Gravity Falls, for instance, demonstrates how mystery and supernatural adventures can operate alongside comedy. Avatar: The Last Airbender offers an example of serialized storytelling involving character growth, conflict, and meaningful consequences.

However, complexity doesn't automatically make a cartoon better.

A simple story told exceptionally well can be just as satisfying as an elaborate adventure.

Research context: Pixar's storytelling curriculum discusses story structure, themes, character development, and visual storytelling as interconnected parts of narrative design.

3. Effective Humor and Emotional Impact

One of my favorite qualities in animation is its ability to make audiences feel something.

For a comedy, I pay attention to comedic timing, character interactions, and whether the humor fits the show's personality.

For a more dramatic cartoon, I consider whether the emotions feel earned and whether the story gives audiences reasons to care.

I also believe comedy doesn't need to become unnecessarily cruel or offensive to be entertaining.

At the same time, not every cartoon should be expected to maintain a cheerful atmosphere. Dark humor, conflict, and serious subjects can have a legitimate place when handled effectively.

Ultimately, I prefer judging a show's humor and emotional storytelling according to what it's actually trying to accomplish.

4. Expressive Animation and Strong Presentation

Animation isn't merely about making characters move.

It can communicate personality, emotion, energy, and atmosphere through movement and visual design.

Traditional animation principles include timing, anticipation, staging, exaggeration, and squash and stretch. These techniques help animators communicate motion and actions.

Personally, I enjoy cartoons that use their visual style creatively.

However, I don't automatically consider a cartoon superior because it has more detailed backgrounds, smoother movement, or a bigger production budget.

A limited visual style can still be effective when it complements the material.

Voice acting, sound effects, music, and editing also contribute significantly to my viewing experience.

A memorable performance or perfectly timed sound effect can elevate an otherwise ordinary scene.

5. Creativity, Originality, and Lasting Appeal

Finally, I consider what makes a cartoon distinctive.

Does it have an interesting premise? Does it offer memorable experiences? Does its presentation separate it from similar programs?

Originality doesn't necessarily mean inventing something nobody has attempted before. I believe familiar concepts can still become entertaining through thoughtful execution.

I also pay attention to lasting appeal.

If I return to a cartoon years later, does it still entertain me? Do I notice something new? Do its strengths outweigh its weaknesses?

Nostalgia can contribute to enjoyment, but I don't believe nostalgia alone should determine a cartoon's overall quality.

A childhood favorite can be comforting without necessarily being flawless.

My Final Thoughts

Ultimately, there is no single formula that guarantees a great cartoon.

Looney Tunes, Gravity Falls, and Avatar: The Last Airbender approach entertainment differently, yet each illustrates strengths that I appreciate in animation.

I wouldn't expect every comedy to deliver an elaborate dramatic narrative, nor would I expect every adventure series to prioritize nonstop jokes.

For Trace The Rater, a great cartoon is one that understands its identity, uses its strengths effectively, and gives me compelling reasons to keep watching.

And even when I love a cartoon, that doesn't mean I'll ignore its weaknesses.

That's what thoughtful reviewing is all about.
